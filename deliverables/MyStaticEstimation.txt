CS4346 - Improved Static Estimation

1. Features used

Every feature difference below means White's count minus Black's count.
A positive score favors White. For a Black program, the board colors are
swapped before searching, so a positive reported score favors Black.

P: number of pieces.
O: open mill lines containing exactly two own pieces and one empty location.
M: completed mill lines. Each of the board's 18 lines is counted once.
C: mill connections: sum of the number of mill lines through each occupied
   location. For example, a6 belongs to three lines; d0 belongs to one.
T: legal movement routes that would close a mill. A route is an origin and
   a destination, counted once even if it closes two lines. The origin may
   not be one of the two pieces required to remain in the completed mill.
V: legal movement routes before enumerating capture choices. For a player
   with exactly three pieces, every empty destination is available to each
   piece. Otherwise only adjacent empty destinations are available.
B: blocked pieces, meaning pieces with no permitted movement destination.
   Hopping players are not considered blocked merely by occupied neighbors.

O and C are opening features. T, V, and B are midgame/endgame features.
A mill-closing route is not necessarily a capture: under our reading of the
handout, all opposing pieces in mills are protected. T therefore measures
mill-forming opportunity, not guaranteed material gain.

2. Why these features were selected

Material matters because the objective includes reducing the opponent to two
pieces. In the opening, the original evaluator cannot distinguish most
non-capturing placements. Open mills recognize potential captures on a later
placement, and connections reward access to several useful lines.

In the moving phases, merely counting two pieces on a line is insufficient:
a legal move must reach the empty endpoint without removing a required piece.
T accounts for this. Mobility helps avoid being trapped. A blocked piece is
less useful until a route opens. Completed mills describe current structure,
but receive less weight than material and actionable mill-forming threats.

We use a small set of board counts to keep leaf evaluation understandable and
reasonably inexpensive. There is no nested search inside the evaluator.

3. How the features are combined

Opening score = 100*P + 20*O + 10*M + 3*C

Midgame/endgame score = 1000*P + 60*T + 25*M + 5*V - 20*B

Ordinary scores are clamped to [-9000, 9000]. Terminal wins/losses use
+10000/-10000, so positional bonuses cannot outrank a proven game result.
The weights are manually chosen starting weights, not mathematically optimal
or learned coefficients. Material has the largest individual coefficient;
several positional features can still combine to outweigh a material term.

The improved midgame evaluator returns +10000 if Black has at most two
pieces, and -10000 if White has at most two. Search separately checks whether
the player whose turn it is has no legal moves, including at depth zero.
The tournament search also scores a third occurrence of the same board and
side to move as zero when a complete history is supplied.

Opening does not apply the two-piece terminal test while pieces remain to be
placed. The tournament tracks placements separately and switches to the
midgame evaluator after the eighteenth placement, even inside a search line.
The required fixed-phase assignment programs keep the phase specified by
the program name throughout their search, as in the handout interface.

4. Why we believe it improves on the original function

The original opening formula is only piece difference. Our opening formula
can prefer useful development before any capture is available. The original
midgame formula is 1000*piece_difference minus Black's generated move count.
Our formula explicitly measures both players' movement freedom, blockages,
and achievable mill-forming moves. Its movement count does not inflate one
route merely because several different capture targets are available.

Examples.txt supplies two reproducible disagreements at equal search depths,
with the actual chosen boards, scores, and reasons for preferring the new
moves. Numerical scores from different evaluators are not directly comparable.

In four local depth-2 comparison games from two seeded openings, with colors
swapped and the same alpha-beta search for both evaluators, the improved
agent won three games and drew one. Every move was validated and repetition
was tracked. This is a small regression experiment, not proof of tournament
superiority. Validation.txt and validation.json record the experiment and
full histories. We would need broader opponents and time-controlled matches
to make stronger claims.

Rule interpretation

Morris-Variant.pdf page 4 ends GenerateRemove by adding the board when no
removal was possible. We implement this as keeping the mill-forming move
without removing a piece when all opponents are in mills (or none remain).
The PDF's use of the variable b is ambiguous. This interpretation is explicit;
it is not an instructor-confirmed clarification. If the instructor supplies a
different ruling, update generate_remove and its tests, then regenerate all
examples and validation results before submission or official play.
